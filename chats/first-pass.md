The Genesis and Architecture of the Tomato CPU: A Definitive Engineering RetrospectiveIntroduction and Ideological FoundationsThe development of the Tomato CPU—also formalized as the Mosaic-32 architecture—represents a profound departure from standard hardware design methodologies. Designed and built by Tyrone Iras Marhguy, a Ghanaian computer engineering student at the University of Pennsylvania (Class of 2028), the project is a fully custom, 32-bit polymorphic processor built entirely from discrete logic and 74xx series integrated circuits. The system completely reimagines the Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU), bypassing traditional binary logic segmentation in favor of an integrated, dual-LUT (Look-Up Table) datapath that synthesizes Boolean logic directly into the arithmetic stream.To understand the architectural rigor of the Tomato CPU, one must first examine the biographical and philosophical origins of its creator. Born on November 24, 2003, as a triplet alongside sisters Nikita and Amrita, Marhguy's early exposure to electronics was not born out of academic curiosity, but of absolute necessity. For several years, his family lived primarily on a parked bus without electricity in Ghana. At thirteen years old, he provided light and power by charging mobile phone batteries using a car battery and a hand-rewound wind generator. Because the generator lacked functional propellers, the young engineer acted as the wind himself, hand-cranking the system for hours while constantly monitoring a multimeter to keep the voltage just under 5V to safely charge the cells to 4.1V. His first soldering iron consisted of a metal nail heated red-hot in a coal pot, gripped precariously with pliers.This extreme constraint forged an engineering mindset that ruthlessly prioritizes functional verifiability and structural truth. After winning a landmark 2021 High Court case against Achimota School over religious expression (which drew international coverage from the Associated Press and Deutsche Welle), Marhguy achieved a 99th percentile SAT score and secured a full engineering scholarship to the University of Pennsylvania.However, upon entering the standard academic pipeline, the designer encountered a profound philosophical friction with modern computer science education. He observed a system designed to train "human compilers"—students taught to execute procedural algorithms and pattern-match for exams without grasping foundational logic."Either you understand the core logic of the canvas, or you are just memorizing the coordinates of the paint."The frustration of navigating this academic environment while attempting to synthesize complex hardware logic from first principles was isolating. The designer reflected on the psychological weight of the project:"Every second that I spend working on this project, part of me feels like... it's taking too much of my time. And then I go on TikTok and I see the extreme disparity. [...] how many people have sat down to build a computer that can genuinely emulate about 80-something to 100-something ICs fully in a single cycle?"The Tomato CPU was conceived as an antidote to this abstraction. Developed in a university dorm room, the project strips computation down to its bare physical truths. It refuses the "black box" nature of FPGA soft-cores and modern software emulators, forcing the engineer to construct every computational logic gate such that it is physically verifiable on a copper trace.[Image: A composite photograph contrasting the early handwritten schematics from October 2025 on grid paper against the final routed 07_alu PCB design in KiCad.]The Hybrid Hardware Paradigm and the Fused ALUThe earliest schematics of the Tomato architecture appeared on grid paper in October 2025. The fundamental premise was extreme hardware reuse. A handwritten full adder equation read: $C_{out} = 1 \text{ iff } C_{in}(A \oplus B) + AB$, dictating that the carry-out logic must recycle the XOR gates already utilized for the sum generation.Initially, the ambition was to construct the entire 32-bit machine purely from individually hand-soldered discrete MOSFETs. Transistor-level designs using Electric VLSI optimized the textbook NAND/NOR topologies, reducing a single multiplexer's complexity from 66 to 42 transistors. The 8-bit core alone required 3,488 discrete BSS138/BSS84 MOSFETs. While this pure discrete approach successfully passed 1.24 million automated test vectors in Logisim and Verilog simulations, physically soldering nearly 3,500 components introduced unacceptable risks of electrical noise, parasitic capacitance, and signal degradation.To guarantee physical reliability without sacrificing educational verifiability, the architect adopted a Hybrid Architecture. The absolute core of the ALU—the 8-bit Full Adder (368 MOSFETs)—remained fully discrete to preserve the transistor-level rigor. However, the heavy routing logic, including the multiplexers, XOR arrays, and data buses, was migrated to the 74ACT and 74HC series integrated circuits. This dramatically reduced high-frequency noise and created a production-ready footprint.The Single-Cycle adder(lut3, lut3, h(cin)) TopologyThe defining characteristic of the Tomato CPU is its rejection of the "dumb" ALU paradigm. Traditional architectures segregate mathematical circuitry (adders) from logical circuitry (AND, OR, XOR gates), utilizing a terminal multiplexer to select the final output. Tomato32 forces arithmetic and logic to collide in a single, unbroken combinational path.The datapath logic revolves around an integrated formula:$$\text{Result} = f_{\text{LUT}}(A, B, C) + M(B) + C_{in}$$The physical implementation achieves this through three distinct stages:The A-Leg (LUT3): Operand $A$ is routed into an 8:1 multiplexer (the 74ACT151 chip). By dynamically altering the 8-bit control word on the multiplexer's select pins, the hardware acts as a 3-input Look-Up Table (LUT3), capable of executing any Boolean logic function on $A$ before it reaches the arithmetic stage.The B-Leg (Conditioning Block): Operand $B$ is preconditioned by the equation $B_{\text{eff}} = (B \land B_{\text{sel}}) \oplus B_{\text{inv}}$. This structural masking allows the datapath to pass $B$ cleanly, invert it for subtraction ($\sim B$), force it to zero ($0$), or force it to all ones ($-1$).The Adder Matrix: The outputs of the customized A-leg and B-leg, alongside a 16-to-1 multiplexed Carry-In ($C_{in}$), converge simultaneously inside a 74ACT283 fast ripple-carry adder, yielding complex, nested multi-step operations in a single clock pulse.The 1,779 Mathematically Unique OperationsCrossing the 256 physical states of the 3-input LUT with the 4 masking states of the B-leg and the 2 states of the Carry-In yields a raw architectural space of 2,048 permutations ($256 \times 4 \times 2$). However, the architect mathematically proved that this space contains a massive redundancy phantom.Setting the B-leg to $-1$ (all ones) and asserting a Carry-In of $1$ cancels out perfectly to $0$. Therefore, every operation where $f_{\text{LUT}} + 0 + 0$ is executed is structurally identical to $f_{\text{LUT}} - 1 + 1$. Filtering out this duplicate block instantly removes 256 redundant operations. Further isolating edge cases that collapse mathematically (such as subtracting a register from itself always yielding absolute zero) revealed exactly 1,779 non-redundant, mathematically distinct operations natively supported by the copper traces.Filtering this massive dictionary down to the operations that hold tangible real-world value for standard CPU instruction sets leaves a pristine, 81-operation canonical space: 20 unique arithmetic modifications and 61 named logic gates.MnemonicHex CodeA_effB_effCin​Mathematical ResultPrimary ISA FunctionADD0x15$A$$B$$0$$A + B$Standard Ripple-Carry AddADC0x15$A$$B$$1$$A + B + 1$Add with CarrySUB0x1D$A$$\sim B$$1$$A - B$Two's Complement SubtractionRSB0x17$\sim A$$B$$1$$B - A$ARM Reverse SubtractNEG A0x13$\sim A$GND$1$$-A$Two's Complement NegationZERO0x10GNDGND$0$$0$Absolute Zero GenerationMINUS TWO0x1AVCCVCC$0$$-2$Constant GenerationWhen the hardware enters Logic Mode, the adder is essentially bypassed, and the 74151 multiplexers synthesize standard bitwise gates directly.MnemonicLUT HexExpression / Truth TableAND A,B0x88$A \land B$ (Masking/forcing zeros)XOR A,B0x66$A \oplus B$ (Bit toggling)BIC A,B0x22$A \land \sim B$ (Bit clear)MAJ0xE8$AB \lor BC \lor AC$ (Majority gate synthesis)MUX C?B:A0xCA$C ? To achieve this in a single pass, the adder requires the equation $A + A$. However, the original routing matrix on the PCB was asymmetrical: the X-Select array was $[A, \sim A, VCC, GND]$, while the Y-Select array was $[B, \sim B, VCC, GND]$. Because operand $A$ had no physical trace leading to the Y-Select multiplexer, the ALU could not add a register to itself. To resolve this, the designer modified the Y-Select matrix to $[B, \sim B, A, GND]$. By asserting $X=A$ and $Y=A$ concurrently via microcode, the datapath natively calculates $2A$ in one clock pulse.The second challenge was the Absolute Value (ABS) operation. Absolute Value is not a static mathematical state; it is inherently data-dependent. The hardware cannot execute it blindly. The architectural solution required wiring the Most Significant Bit (MSB) of the target data register directly into the control unit. If the MSB is $0$ (positive), the control lines remain static, executing a Pass-Through. If the MSB is $1$ (negative), the control unit dynamically switches the ALU input multiplexers to execute a Two's Complement Negation before the clock edge fires.Memory, Multipliers, and SubsystemsThe Tri-Port SRAM Register FileHigh-performance Digital Signal Processing (DSP) workloads—specifically Multiply-Accumulate ($A \times B + C$) pipelines—frequently suffer from memory bottlenecks. To circumvent this, the designer engineered a register file with immense physical bandwidth, eliminating the sequential read penalty.Constructed from four IDT7134 dual-port SRAM chips arranged in parallel, the 32-bit register file features three independent read ports and one write port per cycle. However, a flat 256-register addressing space would consume an excessive amount of the 32-bit instruction word budget. To maintain a compact instruction footprint, the architecture leverages a banked hardware model. A stateful hardware latch (DIG_D_FF_Nbit_10) manages the bank selection pins, dynamically shifting a 32-register execution window across 16 physical memory banks, providing a total of 512 dynamic registers. Additionally, Register 0 is hardwired to an absolute zero state using a 74HC11 three-input AND gate to gate the data bus output and drop write-enables.Sequential MultiplicationWhile the core ALU executes addition in a single cycle, software-based multiplication via iterative looping would cripple the target 25 MHz clock. The architect explored multiple hardware topologies for a dedicated multiplier. A fully combinational 8x8 Tile approach built from 74HC logic was considered; this would slice a 32-bit multiplication into 16 partial products and resolve the math in exactly 16 clock cycles. A Radix-4 Booth Encoding architecture was also evaluated, processing multiplier bits in chunks of three to save board space.Ultimately, the final datapath implemented a sequential multiplier utilizing an unclocked combinational loop paired with a priority encoder. By shifting and accumulating based on active bits, the hardware achieves a 64-bit output on a 32-bit writeback bus in as little as 1 cycle, and a maximum of 16 cycles.Asynchronous I/O via ESP32 Ring BufferTo handle human inputs (such as PS/2 keyboard signals) without forcing the core CPU into debilitating polling loops, the system architecture delegates peripheral management to an ESP32.The ESP32 is programmed as an intelligent, time-aware First-In, First-Out (FIFO) ring buffer. When a key is struck, a hardware interrupt triggers on the falling edge of the PS/2 clock line to capture the 11-bit serial frame. A C++ structure (KeyPacket) stores the 8-bit scan code alongside a precise millis() timestamp. The ring buffer, configured to a size of 64 entries, automatically tracks the data's lifespan. If a keypress sits in the buffer for more than 5,000 milliseconds without being queried by the CPU, the ESP32 silently drops the packet to prevent memory bloating. When valid data exists, the ESP32 writes the code directly to a dedicated Dual-Port RAM address (0x1FFF), allowing the CPU to read human inputs perfectly synchronized with its internal execution pipeline.PCB Routing OrthogonalityA meticulous detail that highlights the designer's physical engineering prowess is the selection of the bus buffers. In standard digital logic designs, the 74244 buffer is ubiquitous. However, the 74244 features a staggered, zig-zag pinout configuration. In a discrete PCB layout, this physical staggering turns high-speed data buses into a tangled ratsnest.The designer explicitly chose the 74541 Octal Non-Inverting Buffer instead. The 74541 aligns all eight input pins on the left side of the integrated circuit and perfectly mirrors all eight outputs on the right side. This deliberate component selection ensures that the high-frequency PCB traces remain perfectly straight and orthogonal, minimizing routing congestion, mitigating parasitic inductance, and ensuring signal integrity across the primary bus.[Image: A block diagram detailing the tri-port SRAM register file architecture and the ESP32 FIFO ring buffer interconnect scheme.]"ISA as a Wire": The Parametric HypervisorThe most radical software-hardware integration within the Tomato architecture is a concept formalized in August 2026 as "ISA as a Wire". Modern multi-architecture processors often boast support for legacy Instruction Set Architectures (ISAs), but they invariably achieve this through emulation or micro-op translation. They intercept a foreign instruction, decode it through software stored in ROM, and translate it into a proprietary sequence of internal micro-operations.Tomato completely rejects emulation. The datapath treats a foreign ISA as a first-class input—a parametric mapping overlaying directly onto the physical copper multiplexers. The 9-bit opcode of an incoming instruction serves as the literal control word that indexes the MUX select lines, dictating the ALU planes, immediate shapes, and byte lanes in a single physical cycle without any "pre-decode shifter".This mapping relies on three primary architectural pillars:The Overlay Word: The low bits of a standard 32-bit instruction overlay directly onto the copper traces as condition flags or jump modes.The Immediate Box: Physical hardware encodings are built onto the register board to untangle varied immediate formats natively. Whether processing a RISC-V I-type, an ARM rotated literal, or an x86 displacement width, the traces untangle the bits directly without a cycle of bit-packing logic.The Dual-LUT Map: If a foreign instruction mathematically fits into the 3-input Boolean family of the ALU, the system simply loads the target ISA's truth table into the 74151 multiplexers. An x86 integer operation is not emulated; it is mapped identically to a standard LUT load.Through this direct-mapping methodology, adding support for a new ISA is reduced from a microarchitecture redesign to a simple data-entry mapping problem. It does not support unmappable system-level configurations such as x86 segmentation or ARM TrustZone.Evolutionary Hardware: The Self-Synthesizing FSMTo demonstrate the unique sliceable architecture of the CPU, the designer engineered a demonstration protocol that borders on artificial intelligence: an evolutionary Finite State Machine (FSM) that allows the computer to dynamically "invent" its own instructions based purely on desired mathematical outcomes.Rather than reading arithmetic instructions from a static microcode ROM, the system utilizes a Lattice CPLD acting as an FSM. The CPLD injects target input operands into the ALU (e.g., $A=2, B=3$) and expects a specific mathematical output ($Target=5$) pulled from a Test Case ROM. A 16-bit binary counter within the CPLD then sweeps sequentially through all 65,536 possible global opcode permutations for LUT A and LUT B.Attempting this exhaustive brute-force search across a full 64-bit word width would be computationally infinite. To solve this, the FSM isolates the search strictly to a 4-bit slice of the ALU. By the laws of mathematical induction, any truth table that flawlessly executes an operation on a 4-bit boundary is universally guaranteed to scale correctly across 16-bit, 32-bit, or 64-bit cascaded boards.The Gauntlet of 8 Test CasesTo prevent the hardware from experiencing "logic overfitting"—where it might mistakenly lock onto an OR gate that happens to successfully solve the singular test case of $1 + 0 = 1$—the FSM subjects the evolving opcode to eight vicious mathematical edge cases:The Baseline (0000 + 0000): Forces the hardware to respect absolute zero.The Single Bit (0001 + 0001): Mandates basic internal carry generation.The Ripple Trap (0001 + 1111): Forces a violent carry to ripple across all bits and trigger the external $C_{out}$.The Checkerboard (1010 + 0101): Validates the handling of alternating bits with no internal carries.The Max Out (1111 + 1111): Tests maximum saturation threshold logic.The Carry-In Test (0000 + 0000 with $C_{in}=1$): Ensures the slice respects flags cascading from lower boards.The Signed Negative (1000 + 1111): Verifies the Most Significant Bit (MSB) logic behaves under Two's Complement.The Random Noise (0110 + 0011): A standard, unaligned addition to prevent over-optimization for edge cases.The FSM clocks the counter at an intentionally tuned 500 kHz frequency. While relatively slow, a 2,000-nanosecond clock cycle provides a massive engineering safety net against parasitic capacitance across the physical board connectors, guaranteeing that the worst-case 64-bit ripple carry completely settles before the CPLD checks the output.From a theatrical perspective, the sweeping process takes exactly 1.05 seconds. A 16-bit LED array erupts into a chaotic blur as the counter cycles through tens of thousands of truth tables. The moment the output matches the test case across all 8 gauntlets, the Zero flag is asserted. The CPLD drops the write-enable signal, freezing the counter, and permanently burns the discovered instruction into the opcode ROM.Verification, Timing Analysis, and Physics ConstraintsThe translation of theoretical Boolean equations into physical logic gates introduces severe timing challenges. This massive propagation delay is the direct result of the non-pipelined, ripple-carry architecture. The physical printed circuit board, heavily populated with thousands of 74-series ICs and BSS138/BSS84 MOSFETs, suffers from immense parasitic trace capacitance (ranging from 20pF to 40pF per gate) alongside significant trace inductance. This physical capacitance injects an additional 20ns to 40ns of delay into every execution cycle. Consequently, if the discrete system exceeds 30 MHz, the data buses will enter metastability. The optimization allowed the metallic copper traces to shine dynamically beneath a deep green, semi-transparent gloss in the 3D viewer, perfectly preserving the physical reality of the board in a digital space.ConclusionThe Tomato CPU represents a masterclass in foundational hardware engineering, achieved through an unflinching demand for structural understanding. By rejecting the abstraction layers that define modern computer science education, the architecture strips computation down to its bare physical truths.The integration of the polymorphic adder(lut3, lut3, h(cin)) datapath achieves unprecedented mathematical flexibility in a discrete package. Through the "ISA as a Wire" paradigm, the hardware effectively transforms a standard logic board into a universal hardware hypervisor, running instructions natively without micro-op translation. Furthermore, the implementation of the inductive 4-bit Evolutionary FSM proves that a CPU can dynamically synthesize its own logic operations with pure mathematical certainty.Despite severe timing penalties inherent to cascaded 74-series logic and physical PCB routing parasitics, the design safely bridges the gap between massive theoretical purity and tangible hardware execution. The transition from handwritten scribbles on grid paper in late 2025 to the fully routed, beautifully optimized 07_alu copper boards of August 2026 is a testament to uncompromised engineering rigor. The Tomato project proves that with sufficient persistence and mathematical clarity, it is still entirely possible to build a multi-architecture computing system from scratch, one logic gate at a time.
