The Genesis and Architecture of the Tomato CPU: A Definitive Engineering RetrospectiveIntroduction and Ideological FoundationsThe development of the Tomato CPU, also formalized in early schematics as the Mosaic-32 architecture, represents a monumental divergence from contemporary microprocessor design paradigms. Constructed entirely from discrete 74xx series logic integrated circuits and fundamentally lacking an opaque microcode translation layer, the Tomato architecture operates as a parametric physical hypervisor. By directly mapping instruction set architectures (ISAs) onto the copper traces of its multiplexers, the processor natively executes foreign binaries in a single cycle without the latency of sequential decode states.This retrospective examines the origin, mathematical foundations, architectural pivots, and physical assembly of the Tomato CPU. Synthesizing exhaustive engineering logs, Git commits, and schematic revisions stretching from late 2025 to August 2026, this report details the transition from theoretical Boolean algebra on grid paper to the physical realization of the 07_alu logic board in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.To comprehend the extreme hardware rigor enforced throughout the design of the Tomato CPU, one must first analyze the background of its architect, Tyrone Iras Marhguy. Born on November 24, 2003, in Ghana as a triplet, the designer's entry into electrical engineering was precipitated by severe survival constraints rather than academic curiosity. Residing on a parked bus without access to the electrical grid, a thirteen-year-old Marhguy engineered lighting and power systems by hand-rewinding a wind generator. Lacking functional propellers, the system required him to act as the kinetic source, hand-cranking the generator for hours while monitoring a multimeter to maintain a sub-5V output, ensuring mobile phone batteries safely charged to exactly 4.1V. His earliest soldering apparatus consisted of a metal nail heated red-hot in a coal pot, gripped with pliers or a flammable wooden handle to melt salvaged solder chunks.These severe physical limitations forged a design philosophy that explicitly rejects hardware abstraction. Following a highly publicized 2021 High Court victory against Achimota School over religious expression, Marhguy achieved a 99th percentile SAT score and enrolled at the University of Pennsylvania (Class of 2028). However, the standard computer science curriculum generated profound ideological friction. The academic pipeline overwhelmingly prioritized high-level software abstraction, training students to function as "human compilers" who could execute algorithms without understanding the underlying physical transistors. The Tomato CPU was conceived as an antidote to this phenomenon. The overarching directive of the project was to construct a machine where computation was stripped to bare, verifiable physical truths, entirely avoiding the "black box" nature of FPGA soft-cores and modern software emulators.[Placeholder: A composite photograph contrasting the early handwritten schematics from October 2025 on grid paper against the final routed 07_alu PCB design in KiCad, highlighting the evolution from concept to copper.]Evolution of the Hardware ParadigmFrom Transistors to the Hybrid TopologyThe earliest architectural blueprints of the machine appeared in October 2025 on grid paper, establishing the foundational premise of extreme hardware reuse. A handwritten equation for a full adder dictated that the carry-out logic must recycle the XOR gates already utilized for sum generation: $C_{out} = 1 \text{ iff } C_{in}(A \oplus B) + AB$.Initially, the design pursued a pure discrete MOSFET implementation. Utilizing Electric VLSI, the designer optimized textbook NAND/NOR topologies to reduce a single multiplexer’s complexity from 66 to 42 transistors. The 8-bit core alone mandated 3,488 discrete BSS138 and BSS84 MOSFETs. While this pure transistor approach successfully validated against 1.24 million automated test vectors in Logisim and Verilog, the physical realities of assembling nearly 3,500 discrete components presented catastrophic risks regarding parasitic capacitance, signal degradation, and electrical noise.To guarantee physical viability, the architecture shifted to a hybrid topology. The absolute core of the ALU—the 8-bit Full Adder comprising 368 MOSFETs—remained conceptually discrete to preserve the transistor-level rigor, but the heavy routing logic, including the multiplexers, XOR arrays, and data buses, was migrated to 74ACT and 74HC series integrated circuits. This dramatically reduced high-frequency noise while establishing a production-ready footprint.The early engineering phase of this hybrid model is preserved in an expansive 717-page dossier containing twenty distinct chat logs and documentation exports, detailing the iterative refinement of the datapath. These logs trace the architectural pivots from a rudimentary 4-to-1 multiplexer array to the sophisticated dual-LUT design that ultimately went to fabrication. Throughout this period, development was not strictly confined to a laboratory. The pictorial build log captures the project's portability, documenting instances where the laptop and Verilog netlists were operated on an overcast beach, surrounded by sand beneath a grey sky, reflecting the omnipresent nature of the design process.The Fused Datapath: adder(lut3, lut3, h(cin))The defining mechanical innovation of the Tomato CPU is its outright rejection of the traditional Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) structure. Standard architectures physically segregate mathematical circuitry (adders) from bitwise logic circuitry (AND, OR, XOR), utilizing a terminal multiplexer to select the final output. The Tomato architecture forces arithmetic and logic to collide in a single, unbroken combinational path.This topology evolved rapidly throughout mid-2026. Initially, the design relied on a forest of 4-to-1 multiplexers paired with XOR gates to handle carries. However, the engineering logs from late June record the elimination of the terminal mode multiplexers entirely, migrating the design toward an 8-to-1 multiplexer setup. By July 13, 2026, the architecture was fundamentally upgraded to a dual-LUT topology, executing the following parametric equation in a single cycle:$$\text{out} = \text{adder}(\text{mux}(A_n, B_n, C_n), \text{mux}(A_n, B_n, C_n), \text{mux}(\text{carry / flags}))$$The physical implementation achieves this synthesis through three distinct stages:The A-Leg (LUT3): Operand $A$ is routed into an 8:1 multiplexer, physically implemented via the 74ACT151 integrated circuit. By altering the 8-bit control word on the multiplexer’s select pins, the hardware operates as a 3-input Look-Up Table (LUT3), capable of executing any Boolean logic function natively.The B-Leg (Conditioning Block): Operand $B$ is preconditioned by the structural masking equation $B_{\text{eff}} = (B \land B_{\text{sel}}) \oplus B_{\text{inv}}$. This allows the datapath to cleanly pass $B$, invert it for two's complement subtraction ($\sim B$), force it to zero, or force it to all ones ($-1$).The Adder Matrix: The outputs of the customized A-leg and B-leg, alongside a 16-to-1 multiplexed Carry-In ($C_{in}$), converge simultaneously inside a 74ACT283 fast ripple-carry adder.Mathematical Optimization and Operation MappingCrossing the 256 physical states of the 3-input LUT with the 4 masking states of the B-leg and the 2 states of the Carry-In initially yielded a raw architectural space of 2,048 permutations. Following the July 13 upgrade to the dual-LUT architecture with dynamic carry-select, the theoretical slice space expanded exponentially to 524,288 combinational possibilities.However, mathematical proofs executed during the design phase revealed massive redundancy phantoms within this space. For example, executing an operation where the components resolve to $f_{\text{LUT}} + 0 + 0$ is structurally and mathematically identical to $f_{\text{LUT}} - 1 + 1$. Filtering this dictionary down to non-redundant, mathematically distinct operations yielded exactly 1,779 native instructions. The calculation of Logical Shift Left (Multiply by 2, or $2A$) typically requires the equation $A + A$. However, the initial PCB routing matrix was asymmetrical: the Y-Select multiplexer array lacked a physical trace for operand $A$. By modifying the Y-Select matrix to include $A$, the microcode could assert $X=A$ and $Y=A$ concurrently, natively calculating $2A$ in one clock pulse.A secondary challenge involved the Absolute Value (ABS) operation. Absolute value is inherently data-dependent; it cannot be executed blindly via a static LUT truth table. The architectural solution involved wiring the Most Significant Bit (MSB) of the target data register directly into the control unit. A positive MSB triggers a Pass-Through, while a negative MSB dynamically switches the ALU multiplexers to execute a Two's Complement Negation before the active clock edge fires.Subsystems and Structural ArchitectureThe Tri-Port SRAM Register File and Race ConditionsHigh-performance workloads, specifically Multiply-Accumulate ($A \times B + C$) pipelines, are frequently constrained by memory bottlenecks. To prevent sequential read penalties, the architect engineered a register file utilizing four IDT7134 dual-port SRAM chips in parallel. This 32-bit register file features three independent read ports and one write port per cycle (3R1W), enabling complex fetch operations instantly without stalling the pipeline.To prevent instruction word bloat, the 256-register addressing space leverages a banked hardware model. A stateful hardware latch dynamically shifts a 32-register execution window across 16 physical memory banks. Furthermore, Register 0 is hardwired to an absolute zero state using a 74HC11 three-input AND gate to gate the data bus output and intentionally drop write-enables.During the simulation of this memory architecture, the designer encountered a critical timing race condition regarding the same-cycle read and write mechanics. In a combinational SRAM setup, the addresses dump data onto the buses instantly. The ALU ripples the computation, placing the result onto the write data pins. Because cheap asynchronous SRAM is level-sensitive on the Write Enable (WE) pin rather than edge-triggered (like standard 74HC574 discrete registers), tying the clock high for too long allows the newly written value to propagate back through the read port. This forces the ALU to compute a second time within the same cycle, filling the register with metastable garbage. The system design was heavily modified to ensure that the write-enable functioned as a strict, ultra-short pulse rather than a level hold, ensuring that destructive three-operand operations could execute safely.The Shift Board and Sequential MultiplicationWhile the core ALU executes complex mathematical addition in a single cycle, handling shifts and multiplication required dedicated outboard hardware. The shift logic is processed by a 32-bit barrel shifter constructed from a forest of 74HC157 multiplexers. The hardware natively functions strictly as a right shifter. To achieve a Logical Shift Left (LSL), the hardware executes a bit-reverse, passes the data through the right shifter, and executes a second bit-reverse. The shift board natively handles Logical Shift Right (LSR) and Arithmetic Shift Right (ASR), with Rotate Right (ROR) operations handled by feeding the carry flag back through the adder on a secondary cycle.Software-based iterative multiplication looping would severely cripple the system's performance. Initial drafts considered a massive combinational $32 \times 32$ array, but the sheer physical size of laying out 74HC logic to slice 16 partial products simultaneously would exceed the physical footprint of the rest of the computer combined. Furthermore, occupying the multiplexers to act strictly as AND gates within a spatial array would leave no physical ports available to route the cascading sums, creating an architectural dead end.Consequently, the final datapath implements a sequential multiplier utilizing an unclocked combinational loop paired with a priority encoder. By bypassing zeros dynamically, the hardware shifts and accumulates based solely on active bits, yielding a 64-bit output on a 32-bit writeback bus. This priority encoding allows multiplication to resolve with a worst-case latency of 16 cycles, a best-case latency of 1 cycle, and an amortized performance of approximately 4 cycles. (This pursuit of optimized arithmetic blocks is mirrored in the designer's subsequent projects, such as a BFloat16 multiply-accumulate (MAC) unit hardened for the SkyWater 130nm node on the TinyTapeout 07 shuttle).The 40-Bit Detour and Ideological FallbackOn July 30, 2026, the engineering logs document a severe architectural crisis: the machine's highly powerful ALU was bottlenecked by a cramped 32-bit instruction encoding. Executing operations like $(A \oplus B \land C) + B$ inherently required a fourth operand, necessitating an expansion of the instruction width that simply did not fit within 32 bits.For exactly one day, the project transitioned to a 40-bit canonical machine word. This expansion included a 12-bit instruction, four 6-bit addresses, and a 4-bit bank, creating a unified 40-bit word in fetch, instruction registers, and memory. However, by July 31, the designer initiated a complete ideological fallback to 32 bits. The 40-bit architecture was deemed intellectually clean but represented a "second weirdness on top of a weird ALU". The dark silicon philosophy prevailed: unused potential would remain dormant rather than corrupt the standard 32-bit bus width.The final instruction encoding utilized a shared MSB (Bit 31) acting as a 74HC157 select line to toggle between opcode addressing and data routing. This mechanism established a dual-format ISA without requiring a second decoder ROM, forcing destructive $C=W$ formatting for complex operations while preserving the 32-bit real estate.Asynchronous I/O and Peripheral HandlingTo process asynchronous human input without forcing the primary 32-bit CPU into debilitating polling loops, peripheral management is delegated to an ESP32 microcontroller. Programmed as a time-aware First-In, First-Out (FIFO) ring buffer, the ESP32 captures 11-bit serial PS/2 keyboard frames on the falling clock edge. If a human input sits unqueried in the 64-entry buffer for more than 5,000 milliseconds, it is silently dropped to prevent memory bloating. Valid inputs are written directly into a dedicated Dual-Port RAM address (0x1FFF), allowing the Tomato core to read peripherals natively in sync with its internal execution pipeline. The system also utilizes multiplexed ROM time-sharing for its display; nibbles time-share the ROM, while latches hold individual digits gated by a 5-bit counter, relying on human persistence of vision to replace hundreds of discrete logic gates."ISA as a Wire": The Parametric HypervisorThe theoretical culmination of the Tomato architecture was formalized in mid-August 2026 under the paradigm of "ISA as a Wire". Contemporary multi-architecture processors traditionally execute foreign Instruction Set Architectures (ISAs) through micro-op translation, intercepting external instructions and decoding them via ROM into proprietary micro-operations.The Tomato CPU completely rejects software emulation. The datapath treats external ISAs as first-class inputs mapped directly onto the physical copper multiplexers. The 9-bit opcode of an incoming instruction serves as the literal control word that indexes the MUX select lines, dictating ALU planes and byte lanes in a single physical cycle without any "pre-decode shifter".This parametric mapping relies on three distinct physical pillars:The Overlay Word: Low-order bits of standard 32-bit instructions physically overlay onto the traces as condition flags (COND) or jump modes, utilizing the exact same physical copper for multiple logical roles.The Immediate Box: Physical hardware encodings are placed onto the register board to natively untangle 16 distinct immediate shapes, ranging from RISC-V I-types and ARM rotated literals to x86 displacement widths, completely bypassing cycle-wasting bit-packing logic.The Dual-LUT Map: An incoming x86 or ARM integer operation is not emulated; its Boolean equivalent is simply loaded as a physical truth table into the 74151 multiplexers.By reducing foreign architectural support to a data-entry mapping problem (stored inside a 512-row CSV document that defines the ROM), the discrete Tomato datapath achieves unprecedented single-cycle execution coverage across a vast array of historical and modern CPU families.Architecture FamilyNative Hardware Execution CoverageTomato32 Native100%RISC-V (RV32IM + traps)100%LatticeMico32 (LM32)99%DLX (H&P)99%MIPS32 (MIPS I)98%ARM (AArch32 / Thumb-2)94% / 91%PDP-1193%SPARC V891%6502 / Z80 (Retro emulation)86% / 83%Note: The hardware acts as a physical hypervisor strictly for the compute, shift, and load/store layers; it does not support unmappable system-level configurations such as x86 segmentation or ARM TrustZone.Evolutionary Hardware: The Self-Synthesizing FSMTo demonstrate the unique sliceable architecture of the CPU, the designer engineered a protocol that borders on artificial intelligence: an evolutionary Finite State Machine (FSM) that empowers the computer to dynamically "invent" its own instructions based strictly on desired mathematical outcomes. Early in the project, the designer dismissed executing standard algorithms like the Fibonacci sequence or the Collatz conjecture as trivial demonstrations that would leave the hardware severely underutilized, despite early simulations successfully leaving a Fibonacci value of Q4151657813 in a register at 1:50 a.m..Instead, the system utilizes a Lattice CPLD functioning as an FSM. This CPLD injects target operands (e.g., $A=2, B=3$) into the ALU alongside a target output (e.g., $Target=5$) pulled from a Test Case ROM. A 16-bit binary counter sweeps sequentially through all 65,536 global opcode permutations for LUT A and LUT B at a frequency of 500 kHz.Because attempting an exhaustive brute-force search across a full 64-bit data width would be computationally infinite, the FSM isolates the search strictly to a 4-bit slice of the ALU. Emphasizing a lightweight footprint, the stack relies strictly on Vanilla HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and raw Three.js (v0.169.0 via import map), completely bypassing JavaScript bundlers.Initially, exporting the raw layout directly from KiCad 10.0.4 generated a catastrophic 31.0 MB .glb file containing roughly 41,000 un-instanced pad and trace meshes. When the browser attempted to dynamically run a client-side mergeByMaterial script across these meshes, it caused a massive main-thread freeze. Lighthouse profiling recorded a crippling Total Blocking Time (TBT) of 3,130 ms and a Max Potential First Input Delay (FID) of 3,530 ms, plummeting the site's performance score to just 43.To resolve this bottleneck, the asset pipeline was aggressively optimized using glTF-Transform v4.4.2. The critical engineering insight was identifying that KiCad's default "Texture Atlas / Palette" option was destroying visual fidelity. Executing a palette join fused the metallic copper traces beneath an opaque soldermask layer, rendering them invisible in the 3D viewer.The optimization pipeline was rewritten strictly as: dedup → join(keepMeshes) → flatten → join → weld → prune → sparse → draco. This sequence intentionally kept the core layers (_copper, _pad, _silkscreen, _soldermask, _PCB) completely distinct. The 3.5-second UI freeze was completely eliminated, allowing the physical copper traces to shine brilliantly beneath the digital mask, flawlessly preserving the physical reality of the board in a highly optimized digital space.ConclusionThe Tomato CPU stands as a masterclass in foundational hardware engineering, forged through an uncompromising demand for structural and mathematical truth. By deliberately rejecting the software abstraction layers that define modern computer science education, the architecture successfully strips computation down to physical copper and logic gates.The integration of the polymorphic adder(lut3, lut3, h(cin)) datapath achieves a level of mathematical flexibility rarely seen in discrete packages, collapsing complex DSP algorithms directly into single-cycle hardware execution. Through the groundbreaking "ISA as a Wire" paradigm, the hardware transcends the boundaries of a traditional processor, functioning instead as a universal physical hypervisor capable of executing foreign RISC-V, ARM, and x86 instructions natively without microcode translation. Furthermore, the implementation of the inductive 4-bit Evolutionary FSM proves that a physical machine can dynamically synthesize its own logic operations with pure mathematical certainty.Despite the severe timing penalties inherent to cascaded 74-series logic and the brutal realities of trace capacitance, the design safely bridges the massive gap between theoretical purity and physical reality. The transition from handwritten scribbles illuminated by a hand-cranked wind generator to the beautifully routed, ENIG-plated 07_alu copper boards of August 2026 is a testament to unyielding engineering rigor. The Tomato project conclusively proves that with sufficient persistence and mathematical clarity, it is still entirely possible to build a multi-architecture computing system from scratch, one physical transistor at a time.
