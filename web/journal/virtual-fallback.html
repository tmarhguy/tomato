Virtual Tomato, Not an Invented Answer

Bounded compute keeps the program visible and the execution target explicit.

15 September 2026 · from the historical log

Historical entry: this page records the project at the time. Current facts and execution labels follow the status page.

A greeting is a useful messaging proof, but Tomato’s ALU can do more. The compute preview lets Envelop normalize a supported request, show the bounded program and bytecode, then ask Tomato to execute it.

Each job has eight private registers, 256 private words, at most 32 straight-line instructions, and a mandatory return. Unsupported requests fail instead of producing a host-computed answer labeled as Tomato.
